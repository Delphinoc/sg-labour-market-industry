# Policy note: where Singapore's job losses and job openings sit, 2023–25

*Prepared from MOM Labour Market Survey data (data.gov.sg). Descriptive analysis only; no causal claims.*

## Summary

Job losses and job openings are concentrated in different industries. Retrenchment is highest in ICT, finance, wholesale and retail, professional services and manufacturing, where each retrenched worker is matched by fewer than four vacancies. Openings are concentrated in community and social services, accommodation and F&B, real estate, construction and admin and support, where vacancies far outnumber job losses. At the same time, resignation rates in every industry are at their lowest since 2006.

## Findings

1. **ICT has become the highest-retrenchment industry.** Incidence averaged 20.1 per 1,000 employees in 2023–25, up from 11.3 in 2015–19, and peaked at 24.9 in 2023. ICT's share of all retrenchments rose from 6.3% to 16.0%.
2. **Five industries show a restructuring pattern.** ICT, finance and insurance, wholesale and retail, professional services and manufacturing all have above-median retrenchment and 2.6–3.5 vacancies per retrenched worker.
3. **Five industries are tight.** Community, social and personal services have 48.7 vacancies per retrenched worker, accommodation and F&B 36.3, real estate 21.3, construction 15.0 (up from 1.5 in 2015–19) and admin and support 11.7.
4. **The economy-wide ratio has normalised above its pre-COVID level.** There were 5.6 vacancies per retrenched worker in 2023–25 against 3.8 in 2015–19, after a peak of 17.8 in 2022.
5. **Workers are staying put.** Every industry recorded its lowest average monthly resignation rate of the series in 2025 (services 1.3%, down from 2.2% in 2006; manufacturing 0.9%).

## Implications to explore

- Job losses and vacancies sit in different industries, so career conversion support (from the restructuring industries into the tight ones) may matter more than overall job numbers. Occupation and skills data would be needed to test whether retrenched workers are a realistic fit for the open roles.
- The ICT rise deserves a closer look by occupation and firm size, to separate a post-pandemic correction from a structural shift.
- Low resignations can reflect caution as well as satisfaction. Pairing them with wage-change and vacancy-duration data would help tell these apart.

## Caveats

- MOM rounds retrenchments to the nearest 10 and vacancies to the nearest 100. Small industries (real estate, "others") are noisy.
- Vacancies are a June snapshot, while retrenchments are full-year counts. The ratio is an indicator of pressure, not a matching rate.
- Industry-level data cannot show whether an open vacancy suits a retrenched worker.
